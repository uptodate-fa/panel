Acquired causes of protein C deficiency*
  • Warfarin therapy¶
  • Liver disease
  • Chemotherapy (L-asparaginase)
  • Surgery/trauma
  • Vitamin K deficiency (eg, severe malnutrition, antibiotic use)
  • Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)
  • Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)
  • Acute inflammation
  • Certain severe infectionsΔ
* A diagnosis of hereditary protein C deficiency cannot be made in the presence of one of these disorders.
¶ In patients receiving warfarin, measure protein C levels after cessation of warfarin anticoagulation for at least 2 weeks. If it is not possible to safely discontinue warfarin, heparin may be substituted temporarily.
Δ Protein C levels are not generally affected by infection. However, a particularly severe form of acquired protein C deficiency has been described in certain patients with acute viral, bacterial (eg, meningococcemia), and other infections (eg, malaria).
