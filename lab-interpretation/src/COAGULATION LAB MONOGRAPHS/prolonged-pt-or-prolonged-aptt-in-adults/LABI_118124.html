Initial evaluation of prolonged PT and/or aPTT*
This algorithm is intended for use with additional UpToDate content on clinical use of coagulation tests.
PT: prothrombin time; aPTT: activated partial thromboplastin time; INR: international normalized ratio; HIT: heparin-induced thrombocytopenia; DIC: disseminated intravascular coagulation; CBC: complete blood count; VWD: von Willebrand disease.
* The normal ranges for the PT and aPTT vary by laboratory and reagent/instrument combination. In most laboratories, the normal range of the aPTT is approximately 25 to 35 seconds and for the PT approximately 11 to 13 seconds. Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
¶ Effect of anticoagulants on clotting tests:
  • Vitamin K antagonists (eg, warfarin) – Prolong PT/INR (used for monitoring); may weakly prolong aPTT.
  • Unfractionated heparin – Prolongs aPTT (used for monitoring); increases antifactor Xa activity.
  • Low molecular weight heparins, fondaparinux – May prolong aPTT, increases antifactor Xa activity.
  • Direct thrombin inhibitors (eg, hirudin, argatroban, dabigatran) – Prolong PT/INR and aPTT (aPTT used for monitoring parenteral agents).
  • Direct factor Xa inhibitors (eg, rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban) – Prolong PT/INR and aPTT; increase antifactor Xa activity. Multiple clotting tests may be prolonged during the period of transition from one anticoagulant to another.
  • Fibrinolytic agents (eg, recombinant tissue-type plasminogen activator [tPA]) – Prolong PT and aPTT; antifactor Xa not prolonged.
Δ If blood was drawn from a heparinized venous access device, repeat PT and aPTT using venipuncture from a peripheral vein. If convincing bleeding history despite a normal repeat PT/INR and aPTT:
  • Review medications for antiplatelet agents
  • Identify thrombocytopenia, platelet function defects
  • Test for VWD, if unexplained
◊ The evaluation below presumes that the individual has a normal platelet count and normal platelet function.
§ The cause may be due to a factor deficiency or factor inhibitor, usually an autoantibody. Evaluation of the pattern of the PT, aPTT results can be used to localize the clotting defect to the intrinsic, extrinsic, or common pathway. Autoantibodies against factors VIII, IX, V, or X may prolong both the PT and/or PTT, depending on which factor is involved.
