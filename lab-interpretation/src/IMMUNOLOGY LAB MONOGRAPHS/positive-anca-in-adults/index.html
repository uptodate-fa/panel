INITIAL EVALUATION — 

Testing for the presence of antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (ANCA) is useful to help diagnose ANCA-associated vasculitis, a group of rare, often serious, and sometimes fatal vasculitides that require prompt recognition and therapy. The interpretation of a positive or negative ANCA test will be substantially influenced by the a priori estimated probability of ANCA-associated vasculitis, made on clinical grounds. (See "Clinical spectrum of antineutrophil cytoplasmic autoantibodies" and "Granulomatosis with polyangiitis and microscopic polyangiitis: Clinical manifestations and diagnosis" and "Clinical features and diagnosis of eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA)".)

There remains no standardization for testing for ANCA, including enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and immunofluorescence assay (IFA). Testing strategies depend on local preferences and availability of a high-quality, antigen-specific ELISA:

●IFA is relatively nonspecific, and there may be false-negative and false-positive results.

●A positive ELISA for proteinase 3 (PR3)-ANCA or myeloperoxidase (MPO)-ANCA antibodies is associated with substantially higher specificities and positive predictive values for ANCA-associated vasculitis than the IFA staining patterns to which they usually correspond (cytoplasmic-ANCA [C-ANCA] and perinuclear-ANCA [P-ANCA], respectively).

●Obtain specialty consultation to guide testing, for definitive diagnosis, and for management. Further evaluation of ANCA seropositivity must be considered in the context of the clinical assessment (comprehensive history, physical examination, and initial laboratory testing).

A positive test does not indicate a specific disease. However, the associations with specific ELISA tests can be crucial to understanding the clinical situations. Diagnostic considerations include:

●ANCA-associated vasculitis:

•Microscopic polyangiitis

•Granulomatosis with polyangiitis

•Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (Churg-Strauss)

●ANCA-associated, drug-induced vasculitis:

•Medications – The strongest data are with propylthiouracil, hydralazine, and minocycline. Other implicated drugs in clinical use include sofosbuvir, allopurinol, penicillamine, procainamide, clozapine, phenytoin, rifampicin, cefotaxime, isoniazid, and indomethacin, although causal associations between these drugs and ANCA-associated vasculitis are not well established.

•Recreational drugs – Cocaine alone or adulterated with levamisole (in illegally acquired cocaine).

Most patients with drug-induced, ANCA-associated vasculitis have MPO-ANCA, frequently in very high titers, often with an associated positive antinuclear antibody (ANA). Of the drugs listed above, only levamisole, a common adulterant of cocaine, is associated with dual positivity for MPO-ANCA and PR3-ANCA.

●ANCA-associated nonvasculitic disorders:

•Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and gastrointestinal diseases (ie, inflammatory bowel disease, autoimmune hepatitis) can be associated with a positive IFA and, rarely, with a positive ELISA.

•Bacteremia can cause positive tests for C-ANCA and for PR3-ANCA.

Obtain:

●ELISA for PR3-ANCA and MPO-ANCA, if IFA was performed as the initial assay

●Anti-glomerular basement membrane (anti-GBM) antibodies, by ELISA or multiplex nanobead assays, in patients with kidney involvement and/or alveolar hemorrhage

●ANA and anti-double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) antibody in patients with clinical features of SLE

Patients with a positive ELISA usually require a biopsy of affected tissue (eg, skin, kidney) for definitive diagnosis of ANCA-associated vasculitis. (See "Overview of and approach to the vasculitides in adults", section on 'Diagnostic approach' and "Clinical spectrum of antineutrophil cytoplasmic autoantibodies".)

REFERENCE RANGE — 

There are no standardized reference values for the ELISA tests for ANCA; therefore, the sensitivity and specificity will vary among laboratories. Generally for the IFA, if reported, a titer <1:20 is not clinically significant. Interpretation of a specific abnormal result should be based upon the reference range reported for that result.
