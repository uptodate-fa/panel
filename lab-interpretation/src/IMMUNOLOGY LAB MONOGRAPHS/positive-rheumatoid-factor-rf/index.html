INITIAL EVALUATION  — 

Rheumatoid factors (RFs) are usually ordered to evaluate patients with tender, swollen joint(s) who may have clinical synovitis due to rheumatoid arthritis (RA) or another autoimmune rheumatologic disorder.

Interpretation of a positive RF should be based upon the clinical assessment, specifically, the pretest probability of RA. If ordered in the setting of a low pretest probability of RA (eg, for low back pain), positive results are unlikely to reflect the presence of RA, especially if the titer is low. RFs are nonspecific, occurring in 60 to 70 percent of patients with RA, an estimated 5 to 10 percent of healthy individuals, 20 to 30 percent of people with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), virtually all patients with mixed cryoglobulinemia (usually caused by hepatitis C virus infections), and may be present in patients with many other inflammatory conditions. (See "Biologic markers in the assessment of rheumatoid arthritis", section on 'Rheumatoid factor' .)

It is important to review tests that may be available at the time of initial presentation:

● Complete blood count (CBC) with platelets

● Serum electrolytes, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine, glucose

● Liver biochemical tests, including alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST)

● Urinalysis

Suspected rheumatologic disorder  —  Suspicion of a rheumatologic disorder is based on the history and physical examination with particular attention to a complete joint examination and other signs of systemic autoimmune disease. Inflammatory arthritis (synovitis) involving multiple joints (versus one or two joints), especially if in a "rheumatoid distribution" (symmetric polyarthritis of small joints, including the proximal interphalangeals [PIPs], metacarpophalangeals [MCPs], wrists, and metatarsophalangeals [MTPs]), increases the likelihood of an RF-associated rheumatologic disorder. The pattern of joint involvement and negative tests results usually distinguishes RA from osteoarthritis ( table 1 ). (See "Diagnosis and differential diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis", section on 'Evaluation and diagnosis' and "Articular manifestations of rheumatoid arthritis", section on 'Initial clinical presentation' .)

In patients with clinical synovitis, measure:

● Anti-citrullinated peptide/protein antibody, such as anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP) antibodies (see "Biologic markers in the assessment of rheumatoid arthritis", section on 'Anti-citrullinated peptide antibodies (ACPA)' )

● C-reactive protein (CRP) or elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), which are typically elevated in RA and correlate with disease activity and radiographic progression

Anti-CCP positive  —  If both RF and anti-CCP are positive in a patient with synovitis, the patient likely has RA. However, positive results can occur in other diseases, including several autoimmune rheumatic diseases. Obtain rheumatology consult to confirm the diagnosis of RA and to initiate treatment.

Anti-CCP negative  —  If RF is positive but anti-CCP is negative, RA is still possible, as some patients with RA are RF-positive and anti-CCP-negative. However, other causes of inflammatory arthritis (eg, psoriatic arthritis, acute viral polyarthritis, polyarticular gout or calcium pyrophosphate deposition disease, and SLE) must be excluded. (See "Diagnosis and differential diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis", section on 'Differential diagnosis' .)

For any patient with a positive RF and synovitis, is it reasonable to:

● Test for the presence of antinuclear antibody (ANA):

• Positive ANA occurs in up to one-third of patients with RA and may support the diagnosis of other autoimmune rheumatologic disorders, such as SLE.

• Negative ANA helps exclude SLE and other ANA-associated systemic rheumatic diseases.

● Obtain conventional radiographs of the involved joints (see "Articular manifestations of rheumatoid arthritis", section on 'Imaging' ):

• Joint erosions diagnostic of RA may be observed in patients presenting with symptoms for the first time, although generally appearing only after several months of symptoms.

• Radiographic changes more characteristic of alternative diagnoses may be observed in patients with psoriatic arthritis, spondyloarthropathy, gout, or calcium pyrophosphate crystal deposition (CPPD) disease (eg, chondrocalcinosis).

● Refer for rheumatology consult, particularly in patients with symptoms >6 weeks duration

If there is suspicion of infection or crystalline arthropathy; if the patient has a joint effusion due to monoarthritis, oligoarthritis, or asymmetric arthritis; or if the patient's symptoms have been present for <6 weeks, additional testing may include:

● Synovial fluid analysis to exclude infection or crystalline arthropathy if joint effusion is in a nonrheumatoid distribution (eg, monoarthritis, oligoarthritis, or asymmetric joint effusion)

● Serologic studies for viral infection (eg, human parvovirus B19, hepatitis B and hepatitis C)

Arthralgias without inflammatory arthritis  —  Measurement of RF has little value as a screening test to diagnose or exclude rheumatic disease in either healthy populations, those with nonrheumatic disease ( table 2 ), or those with arthralgias in the absence of objective synovitis. RF produced during acute infections are usually transient and not clinically significant.

In patients with an initial positive RF in the absence of inflammatory arthritis, serial RF results are not likely to be helpful, whether persistently positive or negative on repeat. It is important to consider nonrheumatic disorders associated with positive RF ( table 2 ) and to obtain hepatitis B virus and hepatitis C virus serologies in selected individuals based on clinical assessment.

REFERENCE RANGE  — 

RF antibodies are reported as either positive or negative along with a titer. Low titers refer to positive values that are ≤3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) for the laboratory and assay. High titers refer to values >3 times ULN for the laboratory and assay. The diagnostic threshold for each test depends on the commercial kit. Interpretation of a specific abnormal result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
