IgM: immunoglobulin M; VCA: viral capsid antigen; EBV: Epstein-Barr virus; IgG: immunoglobulin G; EBNA: Epstein-Barr virus nuclear antigen; IM: infectious mononucleosis; CMV: cytomegalovirus; PCR: polymerase chain reaction.
* EBV antibody profile consists of three components: IgM VCA, IgG VCA, and IgG EBNA. Serologies are reported as either positive or negative. Interpretation of a specific abnormal result should be based on the reference range reported with that result.
¶ The results of an EBV antibody profile need to be interpreted within the appropriate clinical context.
Δ Positive IgM and IgG VCA antibodies have a high sensitivity for the diagnosis of IM (97 and 94%, respectively). However, false-positive IgM results may occur with infection with other herpes viruses (eg, CMV) and other illnesses with intense immune response.
◊ IgG antibodies to VCA are usually, but not always, detectable at the onset of clinical illness along with IgM antibodies to VCA because of the long incubation period.
§ IgG antibodies to VCA and EBNA are present for life in patients with prior EBV exposure. If the patient's presentation is consistent with typical features of IM, test for EBV-negative mononucleosis-like illnesses. Obtain CMV IgG and IgM antibodies or CMV PCR, HIV fourth-generation testing, and Toxoplasma gondii IgG and IgM antibodies. Although uncommon in adults, also consider a mononucleosis-like syndrome in association with human herpesvirus 6 seroconversion or adverse reaction to a recent medication. If the patient has isolated chronic fatigue, evaluate for other causes of fatigue. Positive EBV serologies should not be attributed as causal for a fatigue state. These serologies are also not reliable markers of chronic active EBV infection, a rare, progressive hematologic disorder due to prolonged high-level EBV viremia.
¥ IgM VCA antibodies usually wane approximately 3 months after onset of clinical illness and are usually negative by the time IgG EBNA antibodies appear, but sometimes overlap.
‡ IgG EBNA antibodies usually develop after recent infection has resolved in the majority of individuals. However, a minority of immunocompetent individuals never develop EBNA antibodies.
† Immunocompromised individuals may have negative EBV serologies.
