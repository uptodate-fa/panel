ALGORITHM — 

The evaluation of a high prostate-specific antigen (PSA) screening test for prostate cancer depends on the magnitude of the elevation (algorithm 1).

SCREENING — 

Prostate cancer screening is controversial, and men should be involved in making the decision of whether to be screened. If a decision is made to screen for prostate cancer, PSA alone is the most appropriate test. (See "Screening for prostate cancer", section on 'Approach to screening'.)

The traditional definition for an abnormal PSA is a level ≥4 ng/mL (mcg/L), when used for screening. The positive predictive value for a PSA threshold of 4 ng/mL (mcg/L) is approximately 30 percent (ie, slightly fewer than one in three men with an elevated PSA will have prostate cancer detected on biopsy).

The 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors finasteride and dutasteride lower PSA levels, and some experts recommend doubling the measured PSA value to interpret PSA results in men taking these medications. (See "Screening for prostate cancer", section on 'Correction for 5-alpha reductase inhibitor'.)

PSA 4 to 9.9 ng/mL

●Repeat PSA testing after a minimum of four to eight weeks because PSA is also elevated in a number of benign conditions (table 1):

•Defer repeat testing for at least six weeks following an episode of acute urinary retention or resolution of urinary symptoms suggestive of infection.  

•Abstain from ejaculation and from bicycle riding for at least 48 hours prior to a second test.

●Obtain urologic consultation for consideration of additional testing if any of the following are present:

•Follow-up PSA ≥4 ng/mL (mcg/L). (See "Screening for prostate cancer", section on 'Interpretation and follow-up of PSA results'.)

•Rise in PSA >0.5 ng/mL (over any time frame) while on a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor (even if the corrected PSA is <4 ng/mL). (See "Measurement of prostate-specific antigen".)

•Nodule, induration, or asymmetry on prostate examination, regardless of PSA level (if a digital rectal examination is performed).

PSA ≥10 ng/mL — Obtain urologic consultation for consideration of magnetic resonance imaging or prostate biopsy.

REFERENCE RANGE — 

A serum PSA <4 ng/mL (mcg/L) generally is considered to be normal. However, the threshold value is controversial, and men with PSA levels below 4 ng/mL (mcg/L) may have prostate cancer. If the patient takes a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor, a correction factor must be applied to the PSA result for accurate interpretation because such medications are known to lower PSA results.

Additionally, reference ranges can vary depending on the patient population (eg, age, race, and weight), random variation, and clinical laboratory. Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported by the laboratory.
