INITIAL EVALUATION — 

Cancer antigen 125 (CA 125) is used primarily as a biomarker for epithelial ovarian carcinoma (EOC), which refers to carcinoma of the ovary, fallopian tube, or peritoneum. Indications for CA 125 testing include:

●Evaluation of an adnexal mass

●EOC screening in women with a confirmed hereditary ovarian cancer syndrome

●Monitoring response to ovarian cancer treatment

Many benign conditions are associated with an elevated CA 125, particularly in premenopausal women (table 1). However, all women with elevated CA 125 with or without an adnexal mass should be referred to a gynecologist or gynecologic oncologist.

Evaluation of adnexal mass or suggestive symptoms — If the CA 125 is obtained to evaluate an adnexal mass (identified on imaging), refer to either gynecology or gynecologic oncology depending on imaging characteristics. If the CA 125 is obtained to evaluate symptoms suggestive of EOC (table 2), obtain (review) transvaginal ultrasound and refer to gynecology or gynecologic oncology. (See "Approach to the patient with an adnexal mass", section on 'Role of tumor markers and multimodal tests' and "Adnexal mass: Role of serum biomarkers in diagnosing epithelial carcinoma of the ovary, fallopian tube, or peritoneum" and "Adnexal mass: Ultrasound categorization".)

Screening for ovarian cancer in women at increased risk — Screening for ovarian cancer should be limited to women with an identified hereditary ovarian cancer syndrome (breast cancer susceptibility gene [BRCA] or Lynch syndrome) who have not undergone risk-reducing bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (rrBSO). (See "Screening for ovarian cancer".)

All women with elevated CA 125 should have transvaginal ultrasound imaging and be referred to a gynecologist or gynecologic oncologist. Furthermore, women with suspected (table 3) or known hereditary ovarian cancer syndrome should be referred specifically to clinicians with expertise in hereditary ovarian cancer syndromes. (See "Screening for ovarian cancer" and "Cancer risks in BRCA1/2 carriers" and "Overview of hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndromes".)

Monitoring response to therapy — After treatment, women with ovarian cancer should be monitored by a gynecologic oncologist. CA 125 may be used for surveillance, along with history and general physical and pelvic examinations. For practical purposes, different CA 125 assays are considered clinically equivalent. However, each of the CA 125 assays in use today has different test performance. Experts advise that the same assay and same laboratory be used when serial CA 125 testing is performed in an individual patient. (See "First-line chemotherapy for advanced (stage III or IV) epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, and peritoneal cancer", section on 'Evaluation after completion of initial chemotherapy' and "Overview of epithelial carcinoma of the ovary, fallopian tube, and peritoneum", section on 'Posttreatment surveillance'.)

REFERENCE RANGE — 

The normal reference range for CA 125 is ≤35 units/mL, but it can vary by specific assay. When monitoring CA 125 levels, experts recommend using the same assay and the reference range reported for that test. Serum CA 125 levels vary by individual and may increase because of benign or malignant conditions.
