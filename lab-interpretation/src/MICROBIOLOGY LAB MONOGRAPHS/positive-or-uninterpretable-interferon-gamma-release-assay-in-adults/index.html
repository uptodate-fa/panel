INITIAL EVALUATION  — 

Interferon-gamma release assays (IGRAs) test for tuberculosis (TB) infection (newer term for latent tuberculosis) ( table 1 ). The assay should only be obtained in individuals at increased risk of new infection due to Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) exposure or at increased risk of reactivation of prior infection due to the presence of underlying conditions, such as HIV infection and malignancy ( table 2 ). Only those who would benefit from treatment of TB infection should undergo testing, so a decision to test presupposes a decision to treat if the test is positive (see "Tuberculosis infection (latent tuberculosis) in adults: Approach to diagnosis (screening)" ):

● As with the tuberculin skin test (TST), IGRAs cannot distinguish between TB infection and active TB disease and should not be used for diagnosis of TB disease in adults ( table 3 ).

● In contrast to the TST, IGRAs are not affected by Bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccination status or prior infection with most environmental nontuberculous mycobacteria and are preferred for patients with history of BCG vaccination, especially if BCG administration was performed any time after infancy or more than once.

The most commonly used commercial IGRA assays include the QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus (QFT-Plus) assay and the T-SPOT.TB assay. The two assays differ in the way results are reported. The QFT-Plus is reported as positive, negative, or indeterminate. The T-SPOT.TB test is reported as positive, negative, borderline, or invalid. In addition, numeric values (ie, absolute interferon [IFN]-gamma values for QFT-Plus or number of spot-forming cells for the T-SPOT.TB test) are provided. In the approach described below, we define uninterpretable as indeterminate, borderline, or invalid. (See 'Reference range' below.)

IGRA positive  —  Review numeric values. If only slightly above threshold for a positive test, consider repeating the IGRA. Individuals with a small elevation are more likely to undergo spontaneous reversion to negative. (See "Use of interferon-gamma release assays for diagnosis of tuberculosis infection (tuberculosis screening) in adults", section on 'Interpreting IGRA results' .)

● Exclude TB disease – Individuals with a positive IGRA should have prompt evaluation to exclude TB disease. Concomitant infection with HIV or other causes of immune suppression are major risk factors for progression from TB infection to TB disease. Persons with immune suppression may have asymptomatic TB disease, and those with advanced immunosuppression may have atypical presentations of TB disease, such as mediastinal lymphadenopathy, extrapulmonary disease, or subclinical infection with a normal chest radiograph. (See "Initial evaluation of adults with HIV", section on 'Tuberculosis' .)

The evaluation to exclude TB disease includes:

• Detailed history to determine possible recent exposure to TB disease, comorbidities (diabetes, renal disease, HIV) that may influence disease likelihood, and presentation and symptoms of TB disease (eg, cough >2 weeks duration, fevers, night sweats, weight loss).

• Comprehensive physical examination to assess for signs of TB disease.

• Chest radiograph (normal in 85 percent of persons with TB infection but may show dense nodules with calcifications, calcified nonenlarged regional lymph nodes, apical fibronodular scarring, or pleural thickening).

Findings suggestive of TB disease should be investigated using sputum for acid-fast bacilli smear and culture and nucleic acid amplification tests in addition to chest radiographs. (See "Diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis in adults" .)

● Decision for treatment of TB infection – Most patients with a positive IGRA should be treated for TB infection after excluding TB disease. The decision for treatment of TB infection is based on the likelihood of developing TB disease if not treated, balanced against the risk of adverse events if treated. (See "Treatment of tuberculosis infection (latent tuberculosis) in nonpregnant adults without HIV infection" and "Treatment of tuberculosis infection (latent tuberculosis) in nonpregnant adults with HIV infection" .)

Obtain specialty consultation if the reason for testing or the risk of developing TB disease is uncertain. The online TST/IGRA algorithm may help estimate the risk of developing TB disease and the risk of drug-related toxicity based on individual characteristics.

IGRA uninterpretable (indeterminate, borderline, or invalid)  —  Uninterpretable results indicate uncertain likelihood of TB infection due to a problem with laboratory processing or a patient-related factor (eg, immunocompromised). The two commercially available IGRA assays (QFT-Plus and T-SPOT.TB) differ in the way uninterpretable results are defined by the manufacturer. (See 'Reference range' below.)

Patients with uninterpretable IGRA and without findings suggestive of TB disease require repeat IGRA. If the patient has an illness associated with immune suppression, the T-SPOT.TB test is preferable, if available, since it has a lower rate of uninterpretable results in such patients. Consult with the laboratory as needed.

Repeat IGRA positive  —  Exclude TB disease. The decision for treatment of TB infection is based on the likelihood of developing TB disease if not treated, balanced against the risk of adverse events if treated. (See 'IGRA positive' above.)

Repeat IGRA negative  —  TB infection is less likely than in those with a positive IGRA. However, false-negative results can occur because of immunosuppression (eg, HIV or TB disease, anergy, treatment with corticosteroids or biologic agents), faded immune memory, and technical-operational variability. Treatment for TB infection despite a negative IGRA may be considered on an individual basis. (See "Treatment of tuberculosis infection (latent tuberculosis) in nonpregnant adults without HIV infection" and "Treatment of tuberculosis infection (latent tuberculosis) in nonpregnant adults with HIV infection" .)

Repeat IGRA uninterpretable  —  Obtain TST in patients with two uninterpretable IGRA results:

● Positive TST – Patients with positive TST should generally be managed in the same way as a person with a positive IGRA. (See 'IGRA positive' above.)

● Negative TST – Patients with a negative TST should be managed in the same way as a person with a negative IGRA. (See 'Repeat IGRA negative' above.)

Serial testing in health care workers  —  For serial testing in populations exposed to TB, data are insufficient for interpretation of IGRA conversions and reversions, and most studies suggest poor assay reproducibility. In these settings, TST is the test of choice. (See "Tuberculosis infection (latent tuberculosis) in adults: Approach to diagnosis (screening)", section on 'Health care workers in some settings' .)

REFERENCE RANGE  — 

The reference ranges for IGRAs are reported based on the cutoffs established by the test manufacturer. Antigens, testing methods, and interpretation criteria differ between IGRA assays. The QFT-Plus is reported as positive, negative, or indeterminate. The T-SPOT.TB test is reported as positive, negative, borderline, or invalid. Interpretation of a specific abnormal result should be based on the reference range reported for that result and the clinical context in which the test was applied.
