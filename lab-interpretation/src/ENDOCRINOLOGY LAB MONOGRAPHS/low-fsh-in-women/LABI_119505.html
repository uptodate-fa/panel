Initial evaluation of low FSH in women*
This algorithm is intended for use with additional UpToDate content on hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
FSH: follicle-stimulating hormone; hCG: human chorionic gonadotropin; TSH: thyroid-stimulating hormone; A1C: glycated hemoglobin; MRI: magnetic resonance imaging.
* The normal range for FSH varies with the menstrual cycle, ranging from 2 to 9 milli-international units/mL during the follicular/luteal phases and 4 to 22 milli-international units/mL at midcycle. Therefore, in premenopausal women, an FSH concentration that is <2 milli-international units/mL is considered low. In menopausal women, the normal range is generally >30 milli-international units/mL. The upper threshold for a normal FSH concentration is laboratory dependent; cutoff values of 10 to 30 milli-international units/mL have been reported because of use of different FSH assay reference standards and assay methodologies. Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
¶ Combination estrogen-progestin contraception suppresses FSH. Amenorrhea on hormonal contraception may be related to the hormone formulation rather than an underlying problem. Assessment of hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis would require discontinuation of the oral contraceptive for approximately one month (during which time the patient might require an alternative nonhormonal form of contraception).
Δ Refer to UpToDate content on hyperprolactinemia and thyroid disease.
◊ Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea is based upon the findings of amenorrhea, low serum gonadotropins and estradiol, and, usually, evidence of a precipitating factor (exercise, low weight, stress).
§ FSH and estradiol are not routinely measured to monitor hormone replacement therapy. Titrate estrogen dose to alleviate symptoms.
¥ In women with clinical evidence of hyperandrogenism (eg, hirsutism, acne), measure total testosterone to assess for androgen-secreting tumors.
‡ Refer to UpToDate content on adrenocortical tumors and ovarian stomal cell tumors.
