INITIAL EVALUATION  — 

Renin can be measured in terms of its enzymatic activity (plasma renin activity [PRA]) or its mass (plasma renin concentration [PRC]) (see "Assays of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system" ). Renin (PRA or PRC) and plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC) are typically obtained in the evaluation of a patient with:

● A suspected secondary cause of hypertension (eg, primary hyperaldosteronism)

● Unexplained hypokalemia or hyperkalemia

● Adrenal insufficiency, to distinguish primary (high renin, low PAC) from secondary (normal renin, normal or low PAC) adrenal insufficiency

Increased PRA leads sequentially to increased angiotensin II and then increased PAC. Thus, increases in both PRA and PAC are consistent with secondary aldosteronism ( table 1 ):

● Etiology – Common causes of secondary aldosteronism include medications; dehydration; and heart failure, cirrhosis of the liver, or nephrotic syndrome, in which edema is associated with relative intravascular hypovolemia and a subsequent reduction in blood flow to the kidneys. Often the diagnosis is apparent from the history and physical examination. (See "Assays of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system", section on 'Aldosterone high' and "Diagnosis of primary aldosteronism", section on 'Other causes of hypertension and hypokalemia' .)

In hypokalemic, hypertensive patients, causes of secondary aldosteronism may include:

• Diuretic therapy (most common)

• Renovascular or malignant hypertension

• Renin-secreting tumor (rare)

● Review

• Medications that increase renin and, subsequently, PAC:

- Diuretics, including thiazides, loop diuretics (eg, furosemide), and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (eg, spironolactone, eplerenone, drospirenone)

- Vasodilators – dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers, hydralazine, diazoxide, minoxidil, guanethidine

• Laboratory protocol – For patients being evaluated for renovascular or malignant hypertension, it is optimal (but may not be practical) to discontinue the following medications for six or more weeks prior to measurement of PRA and PAC:

- Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (spironolactone and eplerenone)

- Other diuretics, with the exception of low doses of hydrochlorothiazide

• In addition, renin and PAC should be obtained in the morning in a seated ambulatory patient. The patient should be out of bed for at least two hours and seated for at least 5 to 15 minutes before sample is drawn.

● Additional testing – In patients with suspected renovascular hypertension, renal imaging is only indicated if a corrective procedure would be performed if clinically significant renovascular disease were detected. Reasonable alternatives for initial testing include duplex Doppler ultrasonography, computed tomographic angiography (CTA), and magnetic resonance angiography. (See "Establishing the diagnosis of renovascular hypertension" .)

REFERENCE RANGE  — 

The normal reference range is based on a morning blood sample from a seated individual:

● PRA: approximately 1 to 4 ng/mL per hour (0.8 to 3 nmol/L per hour)

● PRC: 8 to 35 mU/L

● PAC with unrestricted salt intake: 5 to 30 ng/dL (140 to 830 pmol/L)

Levels of renin and PAC may be affected by sodium intake, age, time of day, upright posture, and medication use. Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
