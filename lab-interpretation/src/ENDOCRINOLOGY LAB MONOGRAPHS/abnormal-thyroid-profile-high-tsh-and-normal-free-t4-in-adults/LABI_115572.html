Abnormal thyroid profile: High TSH* and normal free T4* in adults (not taking levothyroxine)
This algorithm is intended to be used in conjunction with additional UpToDate content on hypothyroidism and non-thyroidal illness.

T4: thyroxine; TSH: thyroid-stimulating hormone.

* The normal range for TSH in nonpregnant adults is typically 0.5 to 4.5 mU/L, and for serum free T4, approximately 0.8 to 1.8 ng/dL (10.3 to 23.2 pmol/L). Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result. During pregnancy, TSH should be interpreted using population-based and trimester-specific reference ranges for TSH and trimester- and assay method-specific reference ranges for serum free T4. When population-based, trimester-specific reference ranges are not available for TSH, 4.0 mU/L should be considered the upper limit of normal in pregnant women.

¶ In general, TSH should not be assessed in seriously ill patients unless there is a strong suspicion of thyroid dysfunction, since there are many other factors in acutely or chronically ill euthyroid patients that influence TSH secretion.

Δ Some hospitalized patients have transient elevations in serum TSH concentrations (up to 20 mU/L) during recovery from severe nonthyroidal illness.

◊ Some experts measure TPO antibodies in patients with subclinical hypothyroidism to predict the likelihood of progression to permanent overt hypothyroidism and to guide management decisions.
