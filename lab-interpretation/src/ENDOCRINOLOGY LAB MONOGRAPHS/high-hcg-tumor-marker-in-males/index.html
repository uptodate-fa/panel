INITIAL EVALUATION — 

For the evaluation of males with a testicular mass, gynecomastia, or during the evaluation, treatment, and monitoring of testicular tumors, quantitative human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) should be measured using a tumor marker assay (variably called beta-hCG tumor marker assay, total hCG tumor marker assay). This is different than the quantitative beta-hCG measured to detect pregnancy. Some testicular germ cell tumors produce intact hCG, while others produce beta-hCG, alpha-hCG, or other hCG fragments. Tumor marker assays measure all the different forms of hCG. (See "Clinical features, diagnosis, and evaluation of gynecomastia in adults", section on 'Evaluation to determine cause' and "Serum tumor markers in testicular germ cell tumors", section on 'Human chorionic gonadotropin'.)

False-positive elevation of hCG can occur in the following settings:

●Presence of heterophile antibodies or other factors that interfere with the immunoassay for hCG. If suspected, repeat hCG at a different laboratory with a different assay system.

●Hypogonadal states, which may lead to a compensatory increase in pituitary production of hCG as well as the secretion of luteinizing hormone (LH). Repeat hCG after testosterone replacement therapy.

Testicular mass — Testicular tumors usually present as a painless testicular lump or mass. In a male suspected of having a testicular tumor, obtain (review):

●Testicular ultrasound

●Serum tumor markers (in addition to hCG):

•Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)

•Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)

Obtain urologic oncology consultation for further evaluation and management.

Gynecomastia — Once gynecomastia has been diagnosed, careful history, physical examination, and a few diagnostic tests allow identification of the cause in most patients (table 1 and table 2). If the cause is not apparent after the initial clinical assessment and the gynecomastia is of recent onset, painful or tender, or >5 cm, biochemical evaluation typically includes hCG (quantitative tumor marker assay), LH, testosterone, and estradiol.

An elevated hCG concentration suggests a testicular or extragonadal germ cell tumor. Serum hCG concentrations above 10,000 milli-international units/mL in males occur only in germ cell tumors or the rare patient with trophoblastic differentiation of a primary lung or gastric cancer.

Obtain a testicular ultrasound:

●Abnormal (mass identified) – The combination of an elevated hCG and a testicular mass on ultrasound is suggestive of a testicular germ cell tumor. Obtain urologic oncology consultation for further evaluation and management.

●Normal – The combination of an elevated hCG and a normal testicular ultrasound may be consistent with an extragonadal germ cell tumor or hCG-secreting nontrophoblastic tumor. Obtain chest and abdominal imaging and oncology consultation.

Monitoring testicular germ cell tumors — Although serum tumor markers are helpful at the time of initial diagnosis of testicular cancer and for prognostication, important additional utility is for subsequent follow-up of disease status after primary treatment. For hCG monitoring, the same tumor marker assay should be used, as results from different assays may vary. The serum half-life of hCG is 1.5 to 3 days, which is an important consideration in determining the response to therapy.

For males who have completed definitive therapy, close surveillance should continue with serial measurement of quantitative hCG (and other tumor markers [AFP and LDH]). If the patient is no longer under the care of an oncologist, any elevation in hCG in a patient with a history of germ cell tumor should be promptly referred to an oncologist for further evaluation. (See "Posttreatment follow-up for testicular germ cell tumors", section on 'NSGCT' and "Diagnosis and treatment of relapsed and refractory testicular germ cell tumors".)

REFERENCE RANGE — 

The normal reference range for quantitative hCG using a tumor marker assay varies with the assay but is typically less than 5 to 10 units/L. Interpretation of a specific abnormal result should be based on the reference range reported with that result.
