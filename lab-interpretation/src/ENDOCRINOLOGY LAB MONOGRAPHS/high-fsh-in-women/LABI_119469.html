Initial evaluation of high FSH in women*
This algorithm is intended for use with additional UpToDate content on amenorrhea and menopause.
hCG: human chorionic gonadotropin; TSH: thyroid-stimulating hormone; FSH: follicle-stimulating hormone; FMR1: gene responsible for the fragile X syndrome; DXA: dual energy x-ray absorptiometry; AMH: anti-Müllerian hormone.
* The upper threshold for a normal FSH concentration is age and laboratory dependent; cutoff values of 10 to 30 milli-international units/mL have been reported because of use of different FSH assay reference standards and assay methodologies. In essentially all assays, however, an FSH >30 milli-international units/mL is seldom observed except in the presence of a depleted pool of follicles (eg, menopause or the menopause transition). Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
¶ A number of screening tests to assess ovarian reserve are utilized, but no single test is highly reliable for predicting pregnancy potential. Day 3 FSH is widely used for screening ovarian reserve, often in conjunction with estradiol and with an AMH level.
Δ Refer to UpToDate content on hyperprolactinemia, thyroid disease, and/or measurement of hCG.
◊ Menopause: 12 months of amenorrhea in the absence of other biologic or physical causes. Menopausal transition: Change in intermenstrual interval with or without menopausal symptoms (eg, hot flashes, sleep disturbance).
§ Primary ovarian insufficiency refers to the presence of irregular or absent menses, elevated serum gonadotropins, and reduced fertility prior to age 40 years. Patients with primary ovarian insufficiency may have symptoms of hot flashes and/or vaginal dryness.
¥ Patients with gonadotropin-secreting pituitary tumors may have changes in peripheral vision (due to suprasellar extension of an adenoma and compression of the optic chiasm), symptoms of hypopituitarism, and elevated serum prolactin (but <100 mg/mL). Refer to UpToDate content on pituitary tumors for additional information.
​‡ Refer to UpToDate content on adrenocortical tumors and ovarian stromal cell tumors for additional information.
