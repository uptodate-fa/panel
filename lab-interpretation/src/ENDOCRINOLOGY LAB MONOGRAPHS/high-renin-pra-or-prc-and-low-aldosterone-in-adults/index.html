INITIAL EVALUATION — 

Renin can be measured in terms of its enzymatic activity (plasma renin activity [PRA]) or its mass (plasma renin concentration [PRC]) (see "Assays of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system"). Renin (PRA or PRC) and plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC) are typically obtained in the evaluation of a patient with:

●A suspected secondary cause of hypertension (eg, primary hyperaldosteronism)

●Unexplained hypokalemia or hyperkalemia

●Adrenal insufficiency, to distinguish primary (high renin, low PAC) from secondary (normal renin, normal or low PAC) adrenal insufficiency

Patients with high renin and low PAC may have hyperkalemia:

●Etiology – The combination of high renin with low PAC may be due to medications or primary adrenal insufficiency. Transient aldosterone deficiency with hyperkalemia and progressively increasing renin can also follow surgical removal of an aldosterone-producing adenoma (transiently suppressed renin-angiotensin-aldosterone axis). Other causes (eg, inherited disorders of aldosterone deficiency or resistance) are rare (see "Etiology, diagnosis, and treatment of hypoaldosteronism (type 4 RTA)"):

•Medications

-Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors and angiotensin II receptor blockers increase renin and decrease PAC.

-Heparin (both unfractionated and low molecular weight, including low-dose administration for prophylaxis) has a direct toxic effect on the adrenal zona glomerulosa cells and may lead to a substantial reduction in PAC. Renin is usually elevated.

•Adrenal insufficiency – In a patient who has been diagnosed with adrenal insufficiency, renin and PAC are often measured in conjunction with adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) to establish the level of defect. A high renin and low PAC is consistent with mineralocorticoid deficiency, which (along with an elevated ACTH) occurs in primary but not secondary adrenal insufficiency. Additional testing to determine the cause of primary adrenal insufficiency may include (see "Determining the etiology of adrenal insufficiency in adults"):

-21-hydroxylase antibodies (autoimmune adrenalitis is the most common cause of primary adrenal insufficiency in resource-abundant countries)

-Computed tomography (CT) imaging if the etiology is uncertain, to identify infectious diseases (eg, tuberculosis), hemorrhage, or metastases

-Very long chain fatty acids (VLCFAs) in young males with negative 21-hydroxylase antibodies, particularly in those with a family history of neurologic abnormalities consistent with adrenoleukodystrophy

REFERENCE RANGE — 

The normal reference range is based on a morning blood sample from a seated individual:

●PRA: approximately 1 to 4 ng/mL per hour (0.8 to 3 nmol/L per hour)

●PRC: 8 to 35 mU/L

●PAC with unrestricted salt intake: 5 to 30 ng/dL (140 to 830 pmol/L)

Levels of renin and PAC may be affected by sodium intake, age, time of day, upright posture, and medication use. Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
