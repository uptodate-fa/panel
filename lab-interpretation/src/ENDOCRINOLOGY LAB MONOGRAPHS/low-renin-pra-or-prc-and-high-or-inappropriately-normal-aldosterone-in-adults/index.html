INITIAL EVALUATION — 

Renin can be measured in terms of its enzymatic activity (plasma renin activity [PRA]) or its mass (plasma renin concentration [PRC]) (see "Assays of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system"). Renin (PRA or PRC) and plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC) are typically obtained in the evaluation of a patient with:

●A suspected secondary cause of hypertension (eg, primary hyperaldosteronism)

●Unexplained hypokalemia or hyperkalemia

●Adrenal insufficiency, to distinguish primary (high renin, low PAC) from secondary (normal renin, normal or low PAC) adrenal insufficiency

In most patients with primary aldosteronism, the renin is very low or undetectable (PRA <1 ng/mL/hour, PRC less than the lower limit of reference range) and PAC is >10 ng/dL (277 pmol/L). Although hypokalemia is a classic sign of primary aldosteronism, potassium levels are frequently normal (see "Diagnosis of primary aldosteronism" and "Evaluation and management of the adrenal incidentaloma", section on 'Aldosteronomas'):

●Assess for interfering medications:

•A mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, such as spironolactone or eplerenone, can increase renin. If renin is not suppressed in a patient who is treated with one of these medications and who is suspected to have primary aldosteronism, the spironolactone or eplerenone should be discontinued for four to six weeks and renin remeasured.

•Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), and direct renin inhibitors have variable effects on renin in patients with primary aldosteronism. In general, renin is undetectable in patients with primary aldosteronism. In a patient treated with one of these drugs, a detectable renin level does not exclude the diagnosis of primary aldosteronism. On the other hand, a strong predictor for primary aldosteronism is an undetectable renin in a patient taking one of these drugs.

●Review laboratory protocol – Renin and PAC should be obtained in the morning in a seated ambulatory patient. The patient should be out of bed for at least two hours and seated for at least 5 to 15 minutes before the sample is drawn.

PAC ≥10 ng/dL and PRA <1 ng/mL/hour (PRC less than lower limit of reference range) — Patients with PAC ≥10 ng/dL (277 pmol/L) and low renin (PRA <1 ng/mL/hour or PRC less than the lower limit of normal) may have primary aldosteronism. These patients typically have a PAC/PRA ratio >20. Most patients require confirmatory testing. Determine need for confirmatory testing:

●Spontaneous hypokalemia, suppressed renin (PRA <1 ng/mL/hour or PRC lower than the lower limit of the reference range), and PAC ≥20 ng/dL (555 pmol/L) are diagnostic of primary aldosteronism. For patients who meet all three criteria, there is no need for confirmatory biochemical testing.

Obtain adrenal computed tomography (CT) to distinguish a unilateral aldosterone-producing adenoma (APA) or, rarely, carcinoma from bilateral hyperplasia (idiopathic hyperaldosteronism [IHA]). This is important since the treatment options are different for the two disorders. Adrenal venous sampling may be necessary. (See "Diagnosis of primary aldosteronism", section on 'Subtype classification'.)

●For patients who do not meet all three criteria, obtain confirmatory testing, usually in consultation with an endocrinologist. Prior to testing, hypertension should be controlled and hypokalemia corrected (hypokalemia suppresses aldosterone secretion). Confirmatory testing can be performed with (see "Diagnosis of primary aldosteronism", section on 'Confirmation of the diagnosis'):

•Oral sodium loading

•Intravenous sodium chloride loading

•Oral fludrocortisone and salt loading

PAC <10 ng/dL or PRA >1 ng/mL/hour — For patients with PAC <10 ng/dL (277 pmol/L) or PRA >1 ng/mL/hour (PRC within normal reference range), surgically curable primary aldosteronism is unlikely. (See "Diagnosis of primary aldosteronism", section on 'Case detection'.)

REFERENCE RANGE — 

The normal reference range is based on a morning blood sample from a seated individual:

●PRA: approximately 1 to 4 ng/mL per hour (0.8 to 3 nmol/L per hour)

●PRC: 8 to 35 mU/L

●PAC with unrestricted salt intake: 5 to 30 ng/dL (140 to 830 pmol/L)

Levels of renin and PAC may be affected by sodium intake, age, time of day, upright posture, and medication use. Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
