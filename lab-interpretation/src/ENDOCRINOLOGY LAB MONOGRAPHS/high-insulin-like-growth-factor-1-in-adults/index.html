INITIAL EVALUATION  — 

Insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) is a hormone produced in the liver and other tissues in response to growth hormone (GH) that mediates most effects of GH. Serum IGF-1 is measured as the initial test in adults suspected to have GH excess due to acromegaly or to determine if an identified sellar mass is a somatotroph (GH-producing) pituitary adenoma. IGF-1 is monitored after treatment of acromegaly to determine the efficacy of treatment. IGF-1 is also measured to evaluate GH deficiency in adults and children, but in this setting, the IGF-1 is low, not high.

IGF-1 must be interpreted according to the patient's age. In healthy individuals, serum IGF-1 concentrations are highest during puberty and decline gradually thereafter, so values are significantly lower in adults over the age of 60 years than in younger people. Thus, an apparently "normal" value in a patient aged 70 years may in fact be elevated. Several conditions lower serum IGF-1 concentrations, including hypothyroidism, malnutrition, poorly controlled type 1 diabetes, liver failure, renal failure, and oral estrogen use. In these situations, it is possible that the diagnosis of acromegaly could be missed.

Diagnosing acromegaly  —  If a patient has typical clinical manifestations of acromegaly and an unequivocally elevated serum IGF-1 concentration, no further biochemical testing is necessary to confirm the diagnosis. If the clinical manifestations are merely suspicious (rather than classic) or the serum IGF-1 concentration is equivocal, the next step is measurement of GH during an oral glucose tolerance test (see "Diagnosis of acromegaly", section on 'Diagnosis' ):

● Oral glucose tolerance test – Testing should be performed in consultation with an endocrinologist who has expertise in neuroendocrinology. Measure serum GH before and two hours after 75 g glucose administration. Inadequate suppression of GH after a glucose load confirms the diagnosis of acromegaly; the criterion for inadequate suppression is a GH concentration >1 ng/mL.

● Imaging – Once GH hypersecretion has been diagnosed, the next step is magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the pituitary (if not already performed), because a somatotroph adenoma of the pituitary is by far the most common cause of acromegaly ( table 1 ).

Monitoring treatment of acromegaly  —  Following initial treatment of acromegaly, whether by surgery or medication, IGF-1 should be measured in two to three months. It should not be measured sooner because of the long half-life of IGF-1. An elevated level suggests that the treatment has not been sufficient. If the IGF-1 level is normal after the initial treatment, it should be measured again in six months and then annually. A rise in IGF-1 after previously normal values suggests recurrence. For persistent or recurrent disease, obtain consultation with an endocrinologist who has expertise in neuroendocrinology. (See "Treatment of acromegaly", section on 'Monitoring' .)

REFERENCE RANGE  — 

The normal reference range for serum IGF-1 varies with age and with the individual assay. A typical reference range is as follows: 16 to 24 years – 182 to 780 ng/mL; 25 to 39 years – 114 to 422 ng/mL; 40 to 54 years – 90 to 360 ng/mL; and ≥55 years – 71 to 290 ng/mL.

Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
