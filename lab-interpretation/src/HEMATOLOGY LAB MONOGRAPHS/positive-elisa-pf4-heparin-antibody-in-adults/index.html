IMMEDIATE ACTION — 

Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) is an immune-mediated, adverse drug reaction to heparin. HIT antibodies are directed against platelet factor 4 (PF4) complexed with heparin, resulting in a hypercoagulable state of platelet activation and thrombin generation. HIT may cause life- or limb-threatening venous or arterial thrombosis. (See "Clinical presentation and diagnosis of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia" and "Management of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia".)

HIT antibody testing is used to confirm or refute the diagnosis of HIT in patients with an intermediate or high pretest probability of HIT. A positive enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) test should prompt urgent consultation with a clinician who has expertise in the diagnosis and management of HIT. The rationale for urgent specialty consultation includes:

●Determination of the likelihood of HIT (or another reason for the clinical presentation)

●Critical decision making regarding ongoing anticoagulation

The related syndrome of COVID-19 vaccine-induced thrombosis and thrombocytopenia (VITT) is discussed separately. (See "Virus-induced immune thrombotic thrombocytopenia (VITT) and VITT-like disorders".)

INITIAL EVALUATION — 

HIT antibody testing is pursued in patients with an intermediate or high probability of HIT based on clinical findings, which may be quantified by the 4 Ts score (table 1). Most individuals with an intermediate, and many with a high probability 4 Ts score, will not have HIT. Similarly, many individuals with a positive ELISA will not ultimately be diagnosed with HIT. The likelihood of HIT is greater with a higher pretest probability (eg, higher 4 Ts score) and with a higher optical density (OD) on the PF4-heparin ELISA. (See "Clinical presentation and diagnosis of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia", section on 'Evaluation'.)

Patients with renal failure or those who have undergone cardiopulmonary bypass have a high incidence of heparin-induced heparin-PF4 antibodies with unclear clinical significance. In these settings, the diagnosis of HIT is even more challenging and requires specialty expertise. (See "Management of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) during cardiac or vascular surgery", section on 'True HIT versus clinically insignificant anti-PF4/heparin antibodies'.)

If the OD is not reported with the test result, it should be obtained from the laboratory. There is variability among different immunoassays in terms of sensitivity, specificity, and predictive value. There is no universally agreed upon OD value that can be labeled as "positive." The following thresholds are for general guidance.

Strongly positive ELISA (optical density ≥2.00) — An OD ≥2.00 is generally considered confirmatory in patients with an intermediate or high probability 4 Ts score, which correlates with an approximately 10 or 50 percent pretest probability of HIT, respectively.

For patients with a low probability 4 Ts score (risk of HIT <1 percent), laboratory testing is generally not recommended, but if an ELISA has been obtained and the OD is ≥2.00 in an individual with a low pretest probability, obtain a functional test. Functional assays may be helpful if the immunoassay gives an indeterminant result or is discordant with the clinical picture but are not readily available in many clinical centers.

Indeterminate ELISA (optical density 0.60 to 1.99) — An OD between 0.60 and 1.99 may indicate HIT in some individuals but is not sufficient to diagnose HIT in most cases:

●High pretest clinical probability – An OD of 1.50 to 1.99 strongly suggests a diagnosis of HIT in patients with a high pretest probability of HIT (4 Ts score (table 1)). An OD of 0.60 to 1.49 generally requires a confirmatory functional study to establish the diagnosis of HIT. (See 'Functional assays' below.)

●Intermediate pretest clinical probability – For patients with an intermediate pretest probability, obtain a functional study to establish or exclude the diagnosis of HIT. (See 'Functional assays' below.)

●Low pretest clinical probability – For the vast majority of individuals with a low probability of HIT, we do not obtain laboratory testing for HIT. For patients with a low pretest probability, in whom an ELISA was obtained and returns 1.50 to 1.99, obtain a functional assay. For OD values of 0.60 to 1.49, clinical judgment is required to determine the best approach, particularly with OD values of ≥1.00. Testing depends on patient characteristics and the availability and turnaround time of immunoassays and functional assays. (See 'Functional assays' below.)

Functional assays — Functional assays assess the ability of a PF4-heparin antibody to bind and activate platelets and, thus, to cause the clinical HIT syndrome. Functional assays are often used in patients with indeterminate immunoassays or in whom the clinical picture and immunoassay results are discordant. Most institutions do not have an in-house functional assay, but these assays are commonly available as a send-out test. The turnaround time is usually longer than that of an ELISA (eg, days):

●Positive SRA or HIPA – A positive functional assay (serotonin release assay [SRA] or heparin-induced platelet activation [HIPA] assay) typically confirms the diagnosis of HIT.

●Negative SRA or HIPA – A negative functional assay (SRA or HIPA assay) excludes the diagnosis of HIT in almost all cases. Re-evaluate for other causes of thrombocytopenia.

The antiplatelet agent ticagrelor may interfere with functional assays for HIT, making it appear that the test is negative when in fact HIT is present. In patients taking ticagrelor, ticagrelor can either be removed (by preincubating patient's serum with charcoal powder) or neutralized by its antidote before the functional assay. If this is not readily available, the 4 Ts score and the HIT ELISA results (particularly its OD value) should be used to guide recommendations and therapy. (See "Clinical presentation and diagnosis of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia", section on 'Patients receiving ticagrelor'.)

REFERENCE RANGE — 

ELISA immunoassays are reported as either positive or negative, based on a predetermined OD cut-off value established by the commercial laboratory. There is variability among different immunoassays in terms of sensitivity, specificity, and predictive value. Functional assays are reported as either positive or negative. Interpretation of a specific abnormal result should be based on the reference range reported for that result and on the pretest clinical probability of HIT.
