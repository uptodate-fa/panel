Causes of neutropenia*
Infections Congenital
Bacterial
  • Typhoid fever
  • Shigella enteritis
  • Brucellosis
  • Tularemia
  • Tuberculosis

Rickettsial

  • Rickettsialpox
  • Human granulocytic anaplasmosis
  • Rocky Mountain spotted fever

Parasitic

  • Kala-azar
  • Malaria

Viral

  • HIV
  • Epstein-Barr virus
  • Hepatitis virus group (eg, hepatitis A, B, and C)
  • Measles
  • Duffy-null associated neutrophil count (DANC, formerly called benign ethnic neutropenia)¶
  • Familial neutropenia in ethnic groups not typically associated with DANC
  • Congenital neutropenia
Iatrogenic
  • Cytotoxic or immunosuppressive agents (reversible, predictable, dose-dependent effects)
  • Idiosyncratic drug reactions (sudden, unexpected onset, independent of dose)
  • Radiation therapy (eg, total body irradiation, radiation injury)
Systemic disease
  • Rheumatologic disorders (eg, SLE, rheumatoid arthritis)
  • Hematologic malignancies (eg, large granular lymphocyte leukemia, hairy cell leukemia, myelodysplastic syndromes)
  • Aplastic anemia
  • Pancytopenia
Other causes
  • Chronic alcohol use
  • Nutritional (eg, B12, folate, copper deficiency)
  • Immune neutropenia (chronic idiopathic neutropenia, autoimmune neutropenia)

HIV: human immunodeficiency virus; DANC: Duffy-null associated neutrophil count (formerly called benign ethnic neutropenia); SLE: systemic lupus erythematosus.

* Conditions listed in this table are derived from reports in the medical literature and from content in UpToDate accessible in the linked topics on neutropenia.

¶ DANC is associated with ANCs 800 to 1200 cells/microL (0.8 to 1.2 × 109 cells/L). This is a normal neutrophil count for patients who are Duffy-negative. DANC is inherited in individuals of certain ethnic groups (eg, African descent, Sephardic Jews, West Indians, Yemenites, Greeks, and Arabs) and is not associated with increased risk of infection.
