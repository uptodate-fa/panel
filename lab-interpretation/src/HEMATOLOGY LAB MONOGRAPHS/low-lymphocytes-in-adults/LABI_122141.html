Initial evaluation of low lymphocytes in adults*
This algorithm is intended for use with additional UpToDate content on lymphocytopenia.
CBC: complete blood count; WBC: white blood cell; ANC: absolute neutrophil count; ALT: alanine aminotransferase; AST: aspartate aminotransferase.
* Lymphocytes generally constitute 8 to 33% of WBCs in the peripheral blood. The normal reference range for the absolute lymphocyte cell count is usually ≥1000 cells/microL and ≤4000 cells/microL. Interpretation of a specific abnormal result should be based on the reference range reported with that result.
¶ Lymphocytopenia may be the sole abnormality in the CBC and blood smear, or it may be accompanied by abnormalities of other WBC types (eg, neutrophils, eosinophils) and/or other cell lines (red blood cells, platelets). Isolated lymphocytopenia may be an incidental finding, especially in older adults, or related to a variety of conditions. Refer to the UpToDate table on the causes of lymphocytopenia.
Δ Lymphocytopenia may be associated with neutropenia. The degree of neutropenia influences the urgency of the evaluation and the need for hematologic and/or infectious disease consultation. An unexplained ANC <500 cells/microL is a medical emergency. Refer to the UpToDate Lab Interpretation monograph on neutropenia and to the table on neutropenia and infectious risk.
◊ Glucose, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, and electrolytes.
§ These include ALT, AST, bilirubin, and alkaline phosphatase.
¥ HIV may be associated with cytopenias. The evaluation depends on whether patients with HIV are acutely ill/symptomatic or stable/asymptomatic. Refer to the UpToDate Lab Interpretation monograph on positive HIV screening test in adults.
