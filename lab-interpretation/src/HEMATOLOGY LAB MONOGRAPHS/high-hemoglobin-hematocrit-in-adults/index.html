ALGORITHM — 

(algorithm 1)

IMMEDIATE ACTION — 

Polycythemia is a laboratory finding in which there is an increased number of red blood cells (RBCs), along with an accompanying increase in the concentration of hemoglobin (Hgb) in the peripheral blood. Identify patients with polycythemia:

●Females: Hgb >16.0 g/dL or hematocrit (Hct) >48 percent

●Males: Hgb >16.5 g/dL or Hct >49 percent

Promptly evaluate the patient for worrisome clinical findings, including:

●Hypoxia, carbon monoxide poisoning, or other potentially life-threatening reactive cause (table 1)

●Clinical conditions that may be due to increased blood viscosity, including:

•Transient visual disturbance, stroke, confusion

•Myocardial infarction, peripheral arterial occlusion

•Venous thromboembolism

Provide urgent management of associated conditions. Critical interventions (eg, hydration of a hypovolemic patient, management of thrombotic events) should not be delayed while performing evaluation to determine the cause of polycythemia. (See 'Initial evaluation' below.)

INITIAL EVALUATION — 

Findings on history, physical examination, and initial testing help to distinguish relative polycythemia (eg, intravascular volume depletion) and secondary polycythemia (eg, hypoxia, cardiopulmonary processes) from rare primary hematologic processes (eg, polycythemia vera) (table 1). (See "Diagnostic approach to the patient with erythrocytosis/polycythemia".)

Repeat complete blood count (CBC) with differential and platelet count. The urgency of evaluation increases with higher levels of Hgb, Hct and/or the presence of symptoms that may be associated with polycythemia (eg, plethora, pruritus, splenomegaly/abdominal pain). As an example, a patient with Hct ≥60 with pruritus, erythromelalgia (intense, burning pain and/or redness of the extremities), and abdominal fullness should be evaluated promptly because of the likelihood of polycythemia vera and the substantial risk of associated thrombosis or other complication. Conversely, an asymptomatic adult with an Hct of 50 may be evaluated in days to weeks.

For patients with persistent, unexplained polycythemia, obtain:

●Pulse oximetry

●Liver biochemical tests

●Electrolytes, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine, glucose

●Serum erythropoietin (EPO) level

●Urinalysis

If clinical findings do not suggest that volume depletion, hypoxia, or a cardiopulmonary process is the cause of polycythemia, further testing is appropriate. Experts differ regarding the order and extent of testing. Obtain hematologic consultation to guide the evaluation of unexplained erythrocytosis (evaluation may include JAK2 and other gene mutation analysis, bone marrow examination, imaging).

In adults, the normal reference ranges for RBC parameters vary by sex, race, and age (table 2). Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
