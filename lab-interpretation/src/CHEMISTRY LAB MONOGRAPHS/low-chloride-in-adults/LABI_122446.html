Initial evaluation of low chloride in adults*
This algorithm is intended for use with additional UpToDate content on low serum chloride.
BUN: blood urea nitrogen; D5W: 5% dextrose in water; COPD: chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; CNS: central nervous system.
* The normal reference range for serum chloride is approximately 98 to 106 mEq/L (mmol/L). Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported by the laboratory.
¶ Recent infusion of a large volume of a hypotonic solution (eg, D5W) may contribute to hypochloremia and unpredictable water and electrolyte derangement.
Δ If other electrolyte abnormalities are present (typically low sodium and/or high bicarbonate), the evaluation of these abnormalities takes precedence.
◊ A low serum chloride with a high serum bicarbonate may reflect either a primary metabolic alkalosis or a chronic respiratory acidosis (reduced chloride and elevated bicarbonate are due to metabolic compensation to hypercapnia). The diagnosis of metabolic alkalosis can be made on the basis of a persistently elevated bicarbonate, if chronic respiratory acidosis is excluded on clinical grounds. Risk factors for hypercapnia (hypoventilation) include severe COPD (emphysema); obstructive sleep apnea; sedative use; postanesthesia hypoxemia; and CNS, neuromuscular, and/or thoracic cage disorders.
