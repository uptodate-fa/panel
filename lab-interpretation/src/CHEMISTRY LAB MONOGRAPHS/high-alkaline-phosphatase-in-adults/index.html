INITIAL EVALUATION — 

Alkaline phosphatase may be derived from several sites, including liver, bone, third trimester placenta, intestine, and kidneys. During the third trimester of pregnancy, the upper limit of the normal reference range is higher than in nonpregnant adults.

Elevated alkaline phosphatase levels may be due to an obstructed hepatobiliary system or increased bone turnover. Marked elevation (≥4 times the upper limit of normal) suggests cholestatic liver disease. Mild to moderate elevation suggests a hepatic or nonhepatic source (table 1).

Review:

●Liver biochemical tests, including alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and bilirubin

Abnormal liver biochemical tests — If other liver biochemical tests are abnormal, the elevated alkaline phosphatase suggests liver disease (see "Approach to the patient with abnormal liver tests", section on 'Elevated alkaline phosphatase'):

●Perform a history and physical examination to identify potential risk factors for liver disease, clues to the etiology, and signs of chronic liver disease.

●Review medications to identify any drugs that may cause acute and chronic cholestasis. The National Institutes of Health (NIH) maintains a searchable database of drugs, herbal medications, and dietary supplements associated with drug-induced liver injury.

●Perform a right upper quadrant ultrasound to further characterize a cholestatic pattern of abnormal liver biochemical test as extrahepatic (table 2) or intrahepatic (table 3).

The subsequent evaluation depends on whether the ultrasound suggests extrahepatic cholestasis (biliary dilation) or intrahepatic cholestasis (absence of biliary dilation). Testing may include hepatitis B virus and hepatitis C virus serology.

Normal liver biochemical tests — If other liver biochemical tests are normal, obtain:

●Fasting alkaline phosphatase

●Gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT) or 5'nucleotidase (5'-NT)

GGT and 5'NT are usually elevated in parallel with the alkaline phosphatase in liver disorders, but they are not increased in isolated bone disorders. (See "Enzymatic measures of hepatic cholestasis (alkaline phosphatase, 5'-nucleotidase, gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase)".)

GGT or 5'NT elevated — If the GGT or 5'NT is abnormal, the elevated alkaline phosphatase suggests liver disease. (See 'Abnormal liver biochemical tests' above.)

GGT or 5'NT normal — If the GGT or 5'NT is normal, the elevated alkaline phosphatase suggests bone disease. An elevated bone alkaline phosphatase may be caused by several disorders including:

●Healing fractures

●Paget disease of bone (see "Clinical manifestations and diagnosis of Paget disease of bone", section on 'Clinical manifestations')

●Osteomalacia (see "Clinical manifestations, diagnosis, and treatment of osteomalacia in adults", section on 'Diagnostic and etiologic evaluation')

●Osteogenic sarcoma, bone metastasis (see "Epidemiology, clinical presentation, and diagnosis of bone metastasis in adults")

●Hyperparathyroidism (see "Primary hyperparathyroidism: Diagnosis, differential diagnosis, and evaluation")

●Hyperthyroidism (see "Overview of the clinical manifestations of hyperthyroidism in adults", section on 'Bone')

Review tests usually available at the time of diagnosis (electrolytes, blood urea nitrogen [BUN], creatinine, calcium). Additional testing may include measurement of the following levels:

●Bone-specific alkaline phosphatase in selected patients (eg, chronic kidney disease, Paget disease)

●Serum calcium, phosphorus, parathyroid hormone, 25-hydroxyvitamin D

●Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)

Obtain specialty consultation to assist with additional testing. Radiographs may be helpful in certain settings (severe bone pain), to distinguish osteomalacia from multiple myeloma or Paget disease of bone.

REFERENCE RANGE — 

The normal reference range for serum alkaline phosphatase is 30 to 120 units/L. The reference range may vary based on age, sex, race, and clinical circumstances such as pregnancy. Interpretation of a specific abnormal test result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
