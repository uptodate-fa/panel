Initial evaluation of high troponin in adults*
This algorithm is intended for use with additional UpToDate content on troponin and myocardial injury.
cTn: cardiac troponin; ECG: electrocardiogram; CBC: complete blood count; BUN: blood urea nitrogen; ACS: acute coronary syndrome; STEMI: ST-elevation myocardial infarction; MI: myocardial infarction; NSTEMI: non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction.
* High sensitivity troponin assays are preferred to sensitive assays. Interpretation of a specific abnormal result should be based upon the laboratory and the clinical context in which the test was applied.
¶ Prompt diagnosis of ACS is essential for early initiation of treatment before the results of cTn testing are known. By the time the troponin results become available, the patient should have already been evaluated for STEMI, hemodynamic instability, and other potentially life-threatening causes of myocardial injury (eg, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, sepsis).
Δ The timing of the second or third measurement depends on the assay. If using a high sensitivity assay, the next cTn should be obtained in 1 to 3 hours. If using a sensitive cTn assay, the next cTn measurement should be obtained in 6 hours.
◊ In patients with ST elevation, serial troponin measurement is not necessary for the diagnosis of acute MI. However, serial troponin measurement may help detect reinjury if there is a change in clinical status.
§ There is no single threshold high sensitivity cTn value that reliably discriminates NSTEMI from other causes of myocardial injury. The use of serial values to assess for a dynamic rise or fall in concentration, the absolute concentration, and the clinical syndrome should all be integrated by the clinician to make an assessment regarding the cause of myocardial injury.
