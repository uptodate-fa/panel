Causes of high carbon monoxide (carboxyhemoglobin) in adults*
Source of ambient CO exposure
  • Fire-related CO poisoning¶
  • Intentional CO poisoning
  • Inadvertent, non-fire-related CO poisoning
    • Improper ventilation of fuel-burning devices (eg, kerosene burners, charcoal grills, camping stoves, gasoline-powered generators)
    • Motor vehicles operating in poorly ventilated areas (eg, ice rinks, warehouses, parking garages)
    • Motorboat exhaust
    • Underground electrical cable fires
    • Hookah (water pipe) use for vaporizing and smoking cannabis, tobacco, or opium
  • Occupational exposure to CO
    • Welders
    • Garage mechanics
    • Firefighters
    • Diesel engine operators
    • Toll booth or tunnel attendants
    • Organic chemical synthesizers
    • Metal oxide reducers
Source of endogenous production of CO
  • Hemolytic anemiaΔ
  • Sepsis, critical illnessΔ
  • Exposure to methylene chloride (dichloromethane)◊
CO: carbon monoxide.
* Listed in order of decreasing frequency.
¶ Fires are also a common cause of concomitant cyanide poisoning.
Δ Even in the most severe cases, carboxyhemoglobin rarely exceeds 10%.
◊ Methylene chloride is an industrial solvent and a component of paint remover. Inhaled or ingested methylene chloride is metabolized to CO by the liver, causing CO toxicity in the absence of ambient CO.
