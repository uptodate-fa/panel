Initial evaluation of high bicarbonate in adults*
This algorithm is intended for use with additional UpToDate content on acid-base disorders.
HCO3: bicarbonate; BUN: blood urea nitrogen; CBC: complete blood count; ABG: arterial blood gas; ACTH: adrenocorticotropic hormone; COPD: chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; CNS: central nervous system; PCO2: partial pressure of carbon dioxide.
* The normal reference range for total HCO3 is approximately 23 to 30 mEq/L (or mmol/L [SI]), although high HCO3 is often defined as >27 mEq/L. Interpretation of a specific abnormal result should be based upon the reference range reported with that result.
¶ Risk factors for hypercapnia (hypoventilation) include severe COPD (emphysema); obstructive sleep apnea; sedative use; postanesthesia hypoxemia; and CNS, neuromuscular, and/or thoracic cage disorders.
Δ Life-saving interventions should not be delayed while awaiting the results of diagnostic testing.
◊ The compensatory response to acute respiratory acidosis increases the serum HCO3 by approximately 1 mEq/L for every 10 mmHg (1.3 kPa) elevation in the PCO2:
  • A normal arterial pH in the presence of substantial changes in both serum HCO3 and arterial PCO2 is usually indicative of a mixed acid-base disorder.
  • If the serum HCO3 is not as high as expected, then metabolic acidosis may coexist, and the arterial pH may be substantially reduced.
  • If the serum HCO3 is higher than expected, then metabolic alkalosis may coexist, and the arterial pH may be inappropriately normal.
§ The compensatory response to chronic respiratory acidosis increases the serum HCO3 by approximately 3 to 4 mEq/L for every 10 mmHg (1.3 kPa) elevation in the PCO2:
  • A normal arterial pH in the presence of substantial changes in both serum HCO3 and arterial PCO2 is usually indicative of a mixed acid-base disorder.
  • If the serum HCO3 is not as high as expected, then metabolic acidosis may coexist, and the arterial pH may be substantially reduced.
  • If the serum HCO3 is higher than expected, then metabolic alkalosis may coexist, and the arterial pH may be inappropriately normal.
